8. Important gaps vs. your original brief

This is where I would focus if we're going to improve the prototype.

🔴 1. The “voice” input isn't actually voice

The UI says:

“Stands in for a voice note transcript in this prototype.”

So currently:

Voice note
     ↓
❌

Typed text
     ↓
AI

rather than:

Voice note
     ↓
Speech-to-text
     ↓
AI

For a 30-minute MVP, that's acceptable.

For the pitch/demo, however, we should decide whether to fake the interaction elegantly or actually add browser speech recognition.

🔴 2. Photo is NOT used by the AI

The brief's future vision is:

AI Vision Condition Grading

But currently:

Photo → displayed on listing
Text → sent to AI

The AI never sees the uploaded image.

Therefore the displayed:

Condition graded 82%

is currently inferred from the text description, not the photograph.

That's an important distinction.

🟠 3. Category isn't actually constraining the curve

The prompt says:

Category: Food
Description: ...

and lets the model choose:

steep_4hr
linear_24hr
flat_2week

So it is category-aware, but not truly category-trained.

For example, nothing technically prevents the model from deciding:

Bread → flat_2week

The prompt asks it to make a sensible choice, but there is no deterministic category rule or learned model behind it.

For the prototype, that's okay.

🟠 4. No actual “expired” state

Your original data model has:

active
claimed
expired

The implementation only really uses:

active
claimed

When the countdown reaches zero, the listing effectively stays active at its floor price.

So:

0:00
↓
₹floor
↓
still claimable

rather than:

0:00
↓
expired

This isn't necessarily wrong for the MVP, but it's a discrepancy worth deciding on.

🟠 5. The AI pricing is intentionally very loose

The AI is asked for:

“a reasonable estimate in Indian rupees”

There isn't currently a pricing framework based on:

quantity
category
original market price
condition
age
seller type
location

So the AI-generated starting price is more of a demo estimate than a marketplace pricing engine.

That's fine for this prototype.

9. One thing I particularly like in the current implementation

The prototype doesn't just show:

₹500 → ₹400 → ₹300

It explains why the curve exists.

Each card has:

AI curve · Baked goods stale within hours — steep 4-hour curve...

That is strategically important.

Because otherwise someone can look at the product and say:

“Isn't this just a countdown discount marketplace?”

The AI explanation is what helps establish:

Decay Market isn't merely discounting inventory. It's trying to match the rate of price decay to the rate at which the resource loses value.

That is much closer to your pitch thesis.

17. Pricing logic

For this phase, I would not let an LLM completely invent prices.

Use:

Estimated market value
        ↓
Condition adjustment
        ↓
Surplus adjustment
        ↓
Starting price
        ↓
Decay curve
        ↓
Floor price

Example:

Estimated market value = ₹500

Condition = 85%
↓
₹425

Surplus discount = 15%
↓
Starting price = ₹360

Floor = ₹72

The AI can provide the estimates, but your pricing engine should calculate the final price deterministically.

This makes the prototype much more credible.
