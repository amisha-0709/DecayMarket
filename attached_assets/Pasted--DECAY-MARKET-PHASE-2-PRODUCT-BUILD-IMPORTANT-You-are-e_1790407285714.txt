# 1. PRODUCT THESIS

Decay Market is a marketplace for resources that are losing value because they are surplus, approaching expiry, damaged, opened, leftover, or otherwise becoming less useful.

Examples:

* bakery surplus
* prepared food
* flowers
* fabric off-cuts
* opened paint
* damaged/cracked tiles
* construction leftovers
* event surplus
* retail inventory approaching expiry
* other reusable resources

The core product thesis is:

"AI understands what the resource is, estimates its condition and value, and selects an appropriate price-decay curve based on how quickly that resource is losing economic value."

This is NOT simply a discount marketplace.

The core loop is:

LIST → UNDERSTAND → PRICE → DECAY → DISCOVER → CLAIM → PICK UP → RESCUE

The product should make this loop obvious.

# 2. PRIMARY USER TYPES

Support two simple user roles.

## SELLER

A person or business that has surplus/unused/soon-to-be-wasted resources.

Seller goals:

* list quickly
* avoid long forms
* let AI structure the listing
* understand why AI chose the price/curve
* monitor claims
* coordinate pickup
* see value recovered and waste diverted

## BUYER / RESCUER

A person looking for useful surplus resources at a lower price.

Buyer goals:

* quickly discover nearby resources
* understand condition
* understand current price
* understand how quickly the price is changing
* claim easily
* know exactly where and when to collect
* see rescue history

For the prototype, the same person can switch between Seller and Buyer mode.

Do not build complicated authentication.

A simple demo user/session is sufficient.

# 13. AI DECISION LOGIC

The AI should consider:

1. Item category
2. Specific item type
3. Condition
4. Whether the item is perishable
5. How quickly quality/value normally declines
6. Quantity
7. Pickup deadline
8. Whether the resource remains useful after its ideal condition
9. Estimated market value

Do NOT make the curve selection purely category-based.

Examples:

Bread:
usually steep

Fresh flowers:
usually steep

Fabric off-cuts:
usually linear

Opened paint:
usually linear

Cracked but usable tiles:
usually flat

However, the AI may choose differently when the item description indicates a different decay pattern.

The AI must explain its decision.

---

# 14. PRICING ENGINE

Do not allow the LLM to directly calculate the live price every second.

The AI should provide:

* estimated market value
* recommended starting price
* floor price
* curve type
* duration

The application should calculate live price deterministically.

Formula:

progress = elapsed_time / curve_duration

Clamp progress between 0 and 1.

For steep_4hr:

Use a fast-decay curve that reaches approximately 10% of starting price by the end.

For linear_24hr:

Linearly move from starting price toward approximately 10% of starting price.

For flat_2week:

Only reduce approximately 20% across the full 14 days.

The live price must never:

* become negative
* exceed starting price
* fall below floor price

Round INR values to whole rupees.

# 29. DESIGN DIRECTION

Keep the existing visual identity but make the interface more product-like.

Design principles:

1. Current price is the most prominent number.
2. Time remaining is immediately visible.
3. Urgency should be understandable without explanation.
4. AI reasoning should be visible but not overwhelming.
5. Seller flow should feel fast.
6. Buyer flow should feel trustworthy.
7. Use warm urgency states for fast-decaying resources.
8. Use calm/fresh states for early-stage listings.
9. Use responsive layouts.
10. Make mobile interaction excellent.

Avoid excessive gradients, animations, cards inside cards, and unnecessary dashboard complexity.

# 37. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

SELLER:

6. AI extracts item information.

10. AI explains why.
11. Seller reviews and can edit.
12. Seller publishes.
13. Listing appears in marketplace.
14. Price visibly decays.

15. Open marketplace.
16. Filter/browse listings.
17. Open listing detail.
18. Understand current price and savings.
19. Understand condition.
20. Understand why the curve exists.
21. See pickup location and deadline.
22. Claim the listing.
23. See claim confirmation.
24. Receive pickup code.

SELLER:

25. See that listing is claimed.
26. See buyer/pickup information.

SYSTEM:
